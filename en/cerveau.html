UX/UI design · 2026

Trousse Cerveau

RoleUX research, interviews and testing, website mockups
ClientNCET Lab, Université Laval
ContextTeam of two, 15 weeks
Year2026

My contributionInvolved at every stage, I led most of the interviews and tests, then designed all of the website mockups. My teammate took charge of the outreach strategy and the presentation materials.

Overview

Rethinking the Trousse Cerveau experience to improve how it is discovered and adopted.

The toolkit explains neuropsychology in plain language for people living with cognitive difficulties. Since it mostly reaches them through professionals, the project combines a targeted outreach strategy with a website redesign.

Problem

A relevant resource, but hard to discover and adopt.

Despite the lab’s efforts to promote it, the Trousse Cerveau saw little adoption among professionals. The brief focused on the website; research showed the barrier came earlier, when professionals first discover the toolkit.

Research and design

Focus on the people who recommend the kit, then simplify the site.

With 15 weeks, redesigning only the website would have been simpler. But a clearer website is useless if no one knows it exists. The outreach therefore aims to reach and equip professionals; the redesign then makes the website easier for everyone to navigate.

The decisions below are based on 30 months of usage data (May 2023 to November 2025) and on UX research with professionals and people with lived experience in Québec.

Research questionWhat is holding back the discovery and adoption of the toolkit?

1Outreach

Professionals are the main point of entry

People with lived experience rarely discover the toolkit on their own: they rely on a professional to learn about it and use it.

Outreach has to withstand staff turnover

The lab already gave occasional presentations in clinical settings, but the information gets lost when teams change.

“It’s work that always has to be redone: a few months later, the team can be completely different.”NCET Lab member, focus group

Decision: Reach professionals from their training onward and at conferences, so the toolkit travels beyond a single team.

Reaching professionals

Three opportunities make it possible to present the toolkit to as many people as possible.

2Adoption

Knowing about the toolkit isn’t enough to use it

After a presentation, some look at the resource without knowing how to integrate it into their sessions.

Decision: Encourage adoption by professionals with an easy-to-use clinical user guide.

Encouraging adoption by professionals

The website gives professionals a clinical user guide, adapted to several types of practice.

3Navigation

A toolkit with a distinct concept

The homepage said little about the toolkit’s transdiagnostic approach. Because navigation was hidden in a menu, many people missed the pages that explained it.

Decision: Organize the website by need, explaining from the start what the toolkit is and how to use it.

Organizing the website by need

Profile-based sections give way to three paths: discover the toolkit, find strategies or learn about cognitive functions.

Giving direct access to strategies

A secondary path leads there without going through the theoretical content.

Using the profile as a filter

Choosing a profile (teens, young adults, family and friends) no longer blocks entry: it becomes a filter, chosen once, that follows visitors all the way to the strategies.

4Finding info

Visitors search with a specific need in mind

Some participants had a specific term in mind and wanted to get to it directly, rather than decipher a busy diagram or dig through the website.

“I can’t immediately see what I’m looking for; I’d rather have a list.”Young adult, usability test

An A/B test then compared a list of functions below the brain (A) with a version that links them directly to their regions (B), similar to the existing website. Neither version clearly won: participants liked elements of each. The final version combines the link to regions with an easy-to-scan list.

Decision: Adapt the interface to the target audience: a clear list, without losing the link to the brain.

Adapting the interface to the target audience

A simple list would have been more direct, but the brain gives the toolkit its name and sets it apart from other resources. It is therefore kept, alongside a list of cognitive functions, a more familiar format for finding information quickly.

Next steps

The lab is reviewing the recommendations guide and preparing the website’s new content. It hopes to continue testing with the team once funding is secured.

Next steps: a social media campaign to broaden outreach, and tests with participants recruited by professionals.
