UX/UI design and visual identity · 2025

Allégo

AwardSelected, Student Design Competition, HCI International 2026

RoleUX research, visual identity, UI design
ContextTeam of four, 13 weeks
Year2025

My contributionAs part of the team, I took part in the research and in developing the app concept. I then created the visual identity, the mockups and the promotional video on my own.

Overview

Simplify lesson planning.

Allégo is designed for preschool and elementary teachers. The platform draws on the curriculum, the classroom’s materials and vetted activities to suggest three suitable activities for each open period.

Problem

Teaching is only the visible part of the job.

Outside teaching hours, administrative work and managing students already take a lot of time and energy. Building a bank of classroom activities takes even more, often in the evenings and on weekends.

Research and design

Lighten planning with a tool built for the classroom.

Allégo helps teachers plan more efficiently by bringing the resources they need together in one place.

More than 160 teachers from across Québec took part in the research. The team also went into the field to observe classrooms and talk with teachers, to understand their reality and what could help them.

Research questionWhere can we lighten teachers’ workload?

Invisible overload has become normal

Preparing materials, managing transitions and adjusting activities extend the work well beyond teaching hours.

1Resources

Experience makes up for scattered resources

Experienced teachers gradually build their own bank of activities; others have to search and adapt. A student teacher in kindergarten explained that she even has to buy her own materials, for lack of budget.

“Having everything in one place would make things so much easier. When it’s all together, it saves time: more time to create and choose activities. You click and you have what you need.”Teacher, focus group

Decision: Bring resources together in one tool, taking into account the materials actually available in the classroom.

Bringing resources together in one tool

The curriculum, the classroom’s materials and vetted activities serve as the basis for ready-to-use suggestions.

Taking stock of available materials

Teachers simply take a photo of their materials, ideally laid out on a table: the app recognizes them and adds them to the inventory, where the teacher confirms the quantities.

2Planning

Planning is where needs converge

Some needs, like support with challenging behaviour, are beyond what any tool can address. Activity planning, on the other hand, takes time and is poorly supported by existing tools.

Decision: Lighten activity planning, a task where a tool can genuinely help.

Seeing the nine-day cycle

My cycle shows all nine days at a glance. Colors distinguish subjects, specialist periods and the periods still to be filled.

Planning at your own pace

The platform adapts to the way each teacher works: cycle length, planning preferences and the needs of their group. It can also reuse the activities they already have, if they wish.

3Choice

How many options to offer?

Tests with four participants compared decision time with 15, 3 and 2 options. More options make choosing take longer, but participants wanted to keep the feeling of having a choice.

Decision: Choose from three suggestions: a faster selection that still leaves a sense of choice.

Choosing from three suggestions

For each open period, the assistant suggests three activities matched to the grade level, duration, curriculum and materials. The final choice always rests with the teacher.

Visual identity

A brand that lightens the load, right down to its name.

The name plays on two meanings: “Allez, go!”, a word of encouragement, and “alléger” (to lighten), which is what the app does for planning. The symbol stacks three shapes in balance, like the tasks to juggle each week.

The deep green evokes growth and calm, and stands apart from the blues of existing school platforms. The interface relies on Inter, a sans-serif typeface designed for screens, simple components and one color per subject.

The identity extends beyond the interface to promote the platform.

Next steps

The visuals were refined after the project to push the research-based decisions further. The number of suggestions was validated with four participants.

Next steps: test the full journey with teachers, measure the planning time saved, then extend the approach to other grade levels.
